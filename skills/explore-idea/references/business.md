# Branch: business

For ideas aimed at customers or the bottom line: new products or ranges,
pricing and promotions, suppliers, stores and channels, operations and cost,
marketing campaigns, brand, UX, websites as a customer experience, and customer
journeys.

This branch has two lenses. Pick the one that matches what the idea is mainly
trying to change, say which in one line, and use the other only where it
obviously matters.

- **Commercial lens:** the idea is about money, range, cost or operations
  (merchandising, buying, pricing, supply, finance, director-level cases).
- **Customer lens:** the idea is about what a customer sees, feels or does
  (marketing, UX and UI, content, service, journeys).

Where a section below splits, follow the matching lens.

## Sharpen: what to find out

Both lenses:
- Who decides whether this happens, and what do they care about most?
- What triggered it: a number, a complaint, a competitor, a hunch?
- What does "this worked" look like, as something that can be measured?

Commercial lens:
- Which number moves: revenue, margin, cost, stock, time, risk?
- Rough size: what is the user's own guess of the upside and the cost to try?
  Ask for their figures before offering any.

Customer lens:
- Which customer, at which moment? Name one segment and one point in their
  journey.
- What do they do or feel now, and what should be different after?

## Thinking moves (for Widen)

Use two or three that fit.

- **Trial before rollout.** One store, one category, one region, one email
  segment, one page, one month. What is the cheapest real test?
- **Who already does this?** A competitor, another industry, another country.
  What can be borrowed and what would not transfer?
- **Reverse it.** Raise instead of cut, remove instead of add, fewer instead of
  more. Does the opposite also work?
- **Customer's eye view.** Walk through it as the customer, step by step. Where
  do they hesitate, drop off or get confused?
- **Follow the money.** Who pays, who saves, who loses margin, and does anyone
  inside the business lose out (a team, a target, a supplier relationship)?
- **Bundle or unbundle.** Could it ride along with something already happening
  (a launch, a season, a site change) or be split into a smaller piece?
- **Second-order effects.** If it works, what breaks? Stock, service load,
  cannibalising another line, staff workload.

## Pressure test

Both lenses:
- What is the realistic downside if it fails, and is it reversible?
- What would a sceptical decision-maker ask first? Answer it.
- What evidence exists, and what is still a guess? Keep the two apart.

Commercial lens:
- Upside and cost as ranges with the user's inputs, labelled `[estimate]` where
  not sourced. Show the break-even point in plain words.
- Dependencies: suppliers, systems, other teams, lead times, seasonality.
- Does it fit the brand and the existing range, or pull against them?

Customer lens:
- Would the customer notice, and would they care enough to act?
- How will it be tested: A/B test, prototype, survey, five customer
  conversations, a landing page?
- Accessibility, clarity and trust: could it confuse or exclude anyone?
- Does it match what the rest of the journey promises?

## Trim targets

The smallest version is usually one test with one segment for a fixed period,
with a single success number agreed before it starts.

## Brief extras

Add: the lens used, the decision-maker, the number that should move (and its
current value if known), the trial design, and the main second-order risk.

## Hand on

For a case that needs approval, suggest turning the brief into a one-page
business case or a short deck. For a customer-facing change, suggest a
prototype or test plan. For high-stakes money decisions, suggest a deeper
research or validation pass before committing.
