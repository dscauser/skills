# Branch: people

For ideas about how people work together: hiring, onboarding, training,
development, recognition, wellbeing, policies, ways of working, process
changes, team structure and internal communications.

## Sharpen: what to find out

- Who is affected, and who is it really for: staff, managers, the business,
  or all three?
- What is happening now that prompted it? A specific moment beats a general
  feeling.
- What would staff say about it after six months if it went well? And if it
  went badly?
- Who has to say yes, and who has to actually do something differently?

## Words to pin down

Common sources of talking past each other. Check the ones the idea uses.

- "Optional": genuinely opt-in, or expected but not enforced?
- "Scheme" or "programme": a one-off, a pilot, or a permanent policy?
- "Flexible": flexible for whom, and within what limits?
- "Manager": line manager, department head, or HR business partner?

## Thinking moves (for Widen)

Use two or three that fit.

- **Ask first.** Could the idea start as a question to the people affected (a
  pulse survey, a few conversations, a pilot team) rather than a decision?
- **Remove before adding.** Is there a meeting, rule, form or step that could be
  dropped instead of adding a new one?
- **Smallest group.** One team, one site or one cohort of new starters first.
- **Make it optional.** What happens if it is opt-in? Who opts in tells you a
  lot.
- **Manager as the channel.** Most people changes land or fail through line
  managers. What do they need to make it work?
- **Seen from the edges.** How does it look to part-time, remote, shift,
  new or long-serving staff, or someone with a disability?

## Pressure test

- Fairness: does it treat any group differently, intended or not?
- Legal and policy: could it touch employment law, contracts, data protection
  or health and safety? Flag it for HR or legal review; do not give legal
  advice.
- Workload: who carries the extra effort, and is it on top of their day job?
- Trust: could it be read as surveillance, cost-cutting or box-ticking? How
  would you explain it in one honest sentence?
- Adoption: why will people still be doing this in three months?
- Measurement: how will you know it helped, without measuring the wrong thing?

## Trim targets

The smallest version is usually a time-boxed pilot with one team, a short
before-and-after check, and an easy way to stop.

## Brief extras

Add: who is affected, who must approve, the fairness and legal points to check,
how it will be communicated in one sentence, and how feedback will be gathered.

## Hand on

Suggest a pilot plan, a short consultation with the people affected, or a
review by HR, legal or whoever owns the relevant policy before rollout.
