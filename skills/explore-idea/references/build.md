# Branch: build

For apps, websites, internal tools, automations, AI agents, browser extensions,
and spreadsheets or manual processes that want to become software.

## Sharpen: what to find out

- Who uses it, and what do they do today instead? (The current workaround is
  the real competitor.)
- Is it for the user, their team, their company's customers, or the public?
- How often does the problem happen, and how painful is it each time?
- What would make them say "this worked" one month after it exists?

## Thinking moves (for Widen)

Use two or three that fit, not all of them.

- **No-code first.** Could a form, a spreadsheet, a template, a shared doc or an
  existing tool's setting do 80% of it?
- **Buy or borrow.** Does a product or open-source project already do this?
  Could the idea be a plugin or add-on to something people already use?
- **Shrink the user.** Build it for one person (often the user) first. What does
  the version for exactly one user look like?
- **Swap the interface.** Same job as a chat bot, a browser extension, an email,
  a scheduled report, a CLI or a mobile shortcut. Which fits where the user
  already is?
- **Automate the step, not the job.** Which single step is most tedious? Could
  that one step be the whole product?
- **Flip who does the work.** Could AI, the customer or a supplier do the step
  instead?

## Pressure test

- Does a real alternative already exist? If so, why would anyone switch?
- Who maintains it after it is built, and what does it cost to run?
- Data: what does it need, who owns it, and is any of it personal or sensitive?
- What is the riskiest technical bit, and can it be tried in an afternoon?
- Adoption: what has to change in someone's routine for them to use it?
- For anything public or paid: who are the first ten users, and how do they hear
  about it?

## Trim targets

The smallest version is usually: one user, one job, one screen or one command,
existing data, no accounts, no settings. State the "aha moment": the first time
the user feels the value.

## Brief extras

Add to the brief: the current workaround, the smallest buildable version, the
riskiest technical assumption, and the aha moment. Say whether look and feel
matter to the idea or whether plain-but-clear is correct.

## Hand on

If the idea is going ahead, suggest a fuller ideation or spec skill if one is
installed (for example `ideate` from idea-to-ship-skills), or a planning pass
before any code.
